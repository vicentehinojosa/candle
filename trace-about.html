The Problem

Every detection tool is asking the wrong question.

Did AI write this? — that question is becoming unanswerable, and institutions know it. As generation improves, fingerprinting fails. Watermarks are stripped. Detection collapses under legal pressure. Institutions that built policy on detection are left with nothing.

The meaningful question is whether the work carries a traceable chain of original human thought — whether the human was intellectually present in the production of what they submitted.

The Trace evaluates conversation logs — the actual exchanges between a human and an AI assistant — against a published philosophical framework called Process-Centered Accountability. It is not looking for AI fingerprints. It is looking for evidence of intellectual agency.

How It Works

Four steps. One reading.

1
Export your conversation
In Claude or ChatGPT, select all and copy your conversation, or use the platform's share and export tools. No account required. No data retained.
2
Paste it into The Trace
One field. Paste the conversation. Add optional context — discipline, work type, stage of production — to give the evaluator the frame it needs.
3
The framework reads the exchange
The PCA evaluator examines how the conversation actually moved — where the human led, where the model led, where agency was exercised and where it was absent.
4
Receive your evaluation
A Human Agency Score (0–100), a plain language rationale grounded in what the exchange actually shows, and specific trace moments evidencing agency or its absence. No hedging. A reading.
The Output

What you receive.

Reading the score.

High
High Human Agency (70–100)
The conversation shows sustained original intellectual contribution. The human redirected the model's framing, introduced knowledge the model did not supply, rejected suggestions in favor of their own direction, or synthesized into positions the model did not originate. The process carries a traceable chain of coherence between the human's thinking and the produced work.
Medium
Medium Human Agency (40–69)
The conversation shows genuine engagement alongside significant reliance on model framing. The human contributed original ideas in places, accepted without interrogation in others. Something is driving the work — the human is present — but the process gap is inconsistent.
Low
Low Human Agency (0–39)
The conversation shows minimal original intellectual contribution. The human accepted the model's framing without meaningful interrogation, or showed no evidence that the work required their specific intellectual presence. The output could have been produced without this particular human engaged in this way.

The Framework

Not a black box.

The Trace applies a published philosophical framework developed in aesthetics, process philosophy, and AI accountability. Process-Centered Accountability begins with a distinction that has consequences for how we evaluate all work in an AI-mediated world.

A property is observable in an artifact without reference to its production. An achievement is constituted by the process that produced it. A work can be clear, original, rigorous — those are properties. It can also be the result of genuine intellectual work — that is an achievement. The output alone cannot tell you which it is.

When AI generates content, the question is not what was produced but where the human exercised determining judgment. The creative locus — the point where the human's specific intellectual contribution changed what was produced — is either present or absent. The Trace finds it.
