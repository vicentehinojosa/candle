The Problem

Every plagiarism detector in the world is asking the wrong question.

They ask: did AI write this? In 2026, that question is unanswerable, and it's becoming irrelevant. Every researcher, student, and professional uses AI tooling. Every document has AI in its lineage somewhere.

The meaningful question — the one institutions actually need answered — is different: was there a thinking human behind this work? Did someone genuinely engage with the ideas, redirect the process, synthesize from a position of real understanding? Or did someone hand a prompt to an AI and submit what came back?

"The forty-thousand-year relationship between trace and maker is breaking down. The Trace reads what remains."

The Trace doesn't detect AI. It evaluates process accountability — whether the conversation log shows a human who was genuinely present in the intellectual work. A high agency score means the human was driving: redirecting, rejecting, synthesizing, originating. A low agency score means the human was largely absent: accepting, forwarding, submitting.

The Old Framework

Did AI write this?

AI detectors are trained on AI outputs. As models improve, outputs become indistinguishable. Detection accuracy is already unreliable — and falling.

Worse: the question punishes AI use rather than evaluating intellectual contribution. A student who used AI thoughtfully to challenge their own thinking gets flagged. A student who copied from a human source passes.

The framework is wrong at the root.

The New Framework

Is there a traceable process?

Process-Centered Accountability evaluates the process that produced the work — not the work itself. The conversation log is the artifact that matters.

A human who redirected the conversation, challenged the model's framing, introduced original concepts, and synthesized across multiple exchanges shows high agency — regardless of how much AI assistance was used.

A human who typed a prompt and accepted the output shows no traceable process — regardless of how polished the result.

How to Use The Trace

1
Export or paste your conversation
Export your AI conversation as a JSON file — from Claude (Settings → Export Data), ChatGPT (Settings → Data Controls → Export), or any other assistant. Or simply paste the conversation text directly into the tool. The complete exchange is more informative than excerpts — include redirections, dead ends, and revisions.
2
Add context (optional, improves precision)
Tell the evaluator the subject or discipline, the type of work (dissertation, policy brief, grant proposal), and the stage of production. Context allows the framework to evaluate the process against appropriate standards for the domain and task.
3
Read the Trace
The evaluator reads the conversation against the Process-Centered Accountability framework — four published papers, a decade of philosophical grounding. It identifies where agency was present or absent, finds specific moments in the exchange that evidence the pattern, and produces a plain-language evaluation.
4
Receive your evaluation
A confidence bar (red / yellow / green), a Human Agency Score (0–100), and a plain language rationale grounded in how the conversation actually unfolded — where agency was present, where it was absent, and what the exchange reveals about the thinking behind the work. No hedging. No probability language. A reading.

The Framework Behind the Tool

The Trace is not a black box. It applies a published philosophical framework developed over a decade of work in aesthetics, process philosophy, and AI accountability.

Process-Centered Accountability begins with a distinction: a property is observable in an artifact without reference to its production. An achievement is constituted by the process that produced it. A painting can be beautiful — that's a property. It can also be the work of a master — that's an achievement. The output alone cannot tell you which it is.

When AI generates content, the question is not what was produced but where the human exercised determining judgment. The creative locus — the point where the human's specific intellectual contribution changed what was produced — is either present or absent. The Trace finds it.
